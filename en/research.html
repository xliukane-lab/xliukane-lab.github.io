Intelligent Manufacturing and Logistics

Equipment failures disrupt production plans, while vehicle positions and incoming jobs change the choices available to a dispatcher. We study how operational data and machine condition can inform the next production or logistics decision.

For chemical product manufacturing, we study how process parameters affect product performance and how digital monitoring and digital twins support process analysis. Our work on equipment covers prognostics and health management (PHM). We also develop industrial agents and investigate how to evaluate and validate domain-specific models under representative operating conditions.

ProjectsThe group contributes to defence research through work on process modelling and manufacturing digitalisation. A separate subproject addresses evaluation and virtual validation of domain-specific models.

Selected studies

Dispatching AGVs as terminal jobs arrive

Each dispatch changes both a vehicle’s position and the waiting time for later jobs. We formulated dispatch as a Markov decision process and trained a multi-agent policy to consider makespan and energy use together. Simulations compared operational and energy performance with benchmark policies across several yard sizes.

Recognising faults across operating conditions

Changes in speed and load alter vibration signals, yet faults recorded under the new conditions can be scarce. MXProto fuses shared and modality-specific features, then adjusts the weights of prototype classifiers. Experiments on benchmark datasets and ablation studies examined how both fusion stages contribute to diagnosis.

Measurement · 2026

Planning vessel deployment and empty-container moves together

When container turnaround is uncertain, assigning vessels before arranging empty boxes can increase leasing costs. We linked both decisions in a two-stage stochastic program and solved it with accelerated Benders decomposition. In the reported numerical experiments, integrated planning reduced average total cost by 0.72% relative to reactive repositioning.

Computers & Industrial Engineering · 2024

Renewable Energy

As a wind front crosses a farm, turbines encounter it at different times. We use that spatial relationship to estimate future winds and power output, then study how turbines can coordinate their operation under uncertain inflow.

When sensors drop out, forecasts must rely on incomplete records. We study how to reconstruct those records, generate possible wind trajectories and bring forecasts into wind farm control. In coordinated yaw control, for example, rotating one turbine changes conditions downstream, so the relevant decision is a farm-level one.

ProjectsNSFC Young Scientists Fund project on spatiotemporal super-resolution scenario generation for renewable energy with missing data. Principal investigator: Xin Liu, January 2023 to December 2025.

Selected studies

Generating alternative futures for a wind farm

A single forecast curve cannot describe the different ways a wind front may travel through a farm. GAPGAN maps turbine histories to a graph and generates alternative wind trajectories from the same observations. Using records from 11 turbines, the study compared five benchmarks and examined trajectory shape as well as scenario diversity.

Keeping wind power prediction intervals ordered

Estimating quantiles independently can place a higher quantile below a lower one. This work first learns the median and then interval lengths, preserving their order. An attention-assisted ordinary differential equation network also produces probabilistic forecasts at finer time resolutions.

Recovering missing turbine measurements

Turbine monitoring systems lose individual readings as well as continuous blocks of data. STF-GAIN estimates missing values from different representations and combines the estimates according to their confidence. The study tested both missingness patterns and examined how imputed data affected downstream prediction.

Intelligent Design

An engineering brief often starts with an incomplete description. We investigate how to recover functional requirements, generate concepts grounded in domain knowledge, and refine candidate designs through human judgement and efficient surrogate evaluation.

In conceptual product design, we organise natural-language requirements around functions and structures so designers can trace the basis of a concept. In large design spaces, we investigate how surrogate models can screen alternatives before the more promising ones receive a detailed evaluation.

ProjectsNSFC General Program on collaborative exploration and creative generation in collective-intelligence design spaces. Principal investigator: Xin Liu, January 2024 to December 2027.

Selected studies

Turning an ambiguous brief into a traceable concept

KnowLoop structures requirements in terms of function, behaviour and structure. A domain knowledge graph and expert supervision then guide concept generation by a language model. Across three water-treatment equipment tasks, expert ratings averaged about 2.8% higher than FBStd over four evaluation criteria.

Advanced Engineering Informatics · 2026

Generating interface palettes for a design theme

A design theme can support many palettes, but the colours still need to work together. CS-Ganerator extracts palettes from images and integrates colour knowledge into a conditional generative model. It filters candidates for harmony, then groups them by properties such as warmth and darkness for designers to compare.

Advanced Engineering Informatics · 2025

Screening two-dimensional layouts with a surrogate

Two-dimensional shelf layouts provide a test of surrogate-assisted design. A surrogate first screens weak candidates, then a tabu list guides a deeper search. The genetic algorithm repairs infeasible layouts as it produces new ones. Evaluation covered 25 simulated instances across five scenarios and two real cases.
